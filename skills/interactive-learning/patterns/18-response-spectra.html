Linear response · Damped oscillator

Why are the width of a resonance, the position of a pole and the decay of a kicked system the same number?

Drive a damped oscillator at frequency \(\omega\) and record \(\chi(\omega)\). The absorption peak, the phase flip across it, the dispersive \(\mathrm{Re}\,\chi\) and the ringing after a kick are all fixed by two poles in the lower half of the complex \(\omega\) plane. Causality is the reason they cannot be anywhere else.

\(\chi(\omega) = 1/(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 - \ii\gamma\omega)\), time dependence \(\ee^{-\ii\omega t}\); unit mass; frequencies in units of the default \(\omega_0 = 1\), times in \(1/\omega_0\)
Source: model knowledge. Standard treatment: Jackson, Classical Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., §7.5 and §7.10 (Lorentz model, causality and Kramers–Kronig relations); Landau & Lifshitz, Statistical Physics Part 1, ch. XII (generalised susceptibility); Chaikin & Lubensky, Principles of Condensed Matter Physics, ch. 7 (response functions, sum rules).
