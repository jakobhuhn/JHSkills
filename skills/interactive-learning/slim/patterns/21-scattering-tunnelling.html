Quantum mechanics · Scattering

How much of a wave gets through a barrier?

A plane wave of energy \(E\) meets one or two rectangular barriers of height \(V_0\). Part of it is reflected and part transmitted, always with \(R + T = 1\). Drag the energy and watch \(T(E)\): exponentially small below the barrier, not quite one above it, and exactly one at resonances.

\(-\psi'' + V(x)\,\psi = E\,\psi\) with \(\hbar^2/2m = 1\), \(V_0 = 10\); \(k = \sqrt{E}\), \(\kappa = \sqrt{V_0 - E}\); solved exactly with \(2\times 2\) transfer matrices
Source: model knowledge. Standard treatment: Griffiths & Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics, ch. 2 (scattering states; the rectangular barrier is a problem there); Cohen-Tannoudji, Diu & Laloë, Quantum Mechanics vol. 1, complement HI; Datta, Electronic Transport in Mesoscopic Systems, ch. 2–3 (transmission and the Landauer formula).
