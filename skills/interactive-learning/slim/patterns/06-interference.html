Wave mechanics · Superposition

Why do more slits give sharper fringes, and where do the fringes go when you look?

Each path contributes a unit arrow in the complex plane, turned by \(\delta\) relative to the previous one. Add the arrows head to tail and square the length: that is the screen intensity. Marking which path the particle took removes the cross terms, continuously.

Unit amplitude per path, \(a_j = e^{ij\delta}\); single-slit envelope ignored; detector states with pairwise overlap \(\gamma = \sqrt{1 - D^2}\)
Source: model knowledge. Standard treatment: Hecht, Optics, ch. 10 (diffraction, many slits); Feynman, Leighton & Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics Vol. III, ch. 1; B.-G. Englert, Phys. Rev. Lett. 77, 2154 (1996) (fringe visibility and which-way information).
